A Focus Friend Alternative for Desktop and Browser

Checked 10 October 2026. Everything said here about Focus Friend is limited to platform availability, account requirements and offline behaviour — the kind of fact that doesn't change week to week.

Focus Friend, by Hank Green, is Google Play's App of the Year and reached #1 on the App Store. It's a good app. It's also iPhone, iPad and Android only — there's no web version and no desktop app. If the thing pulling your attention away from work is a browser tab, not your phone, nothing about Focus Friend can help, because it isn't built to be open on the same screen you're working on.

Why there's no Focus Friend for your computer

Focus Friend's whole mechanic runs on your phone staying put. Leave it alone during a session and your bean stays calm and knits; pick your phone up and wander off, and the bean notices. That's a clever answer to phone-driven distraction, and it only works as a phone app — there's nothing for it to watch if you're sitting at a laptop with the phone face-down across the room.

For anyone whose actual problem is a browser — another tab, another site, the thing you told yourself you'd check "just for a second" — a phone-monitoring app has nothing to monitor. The distraction and the tool live in different places.

What PomoMons does instead

PomoMons is a Pomodoro timer that runs in the browser tab you already have open. Sessions default to 30 minutes with a 5-minute short break and a 15-minute long break every fourth session, and every length is adjustable. Finish a focus session and a wild Pomomon — a food-themed pixel monster — shows up right there on screen; throw a tomato and it joins your collection. There are 30 forms across 16 species, with evolutions and rare shiny and dark variants to find.

No account, no install, no ads, free with no paid tier. It works offline once loaded and can be installed to your desktop or home screen if you want an app-like window, but it never requires that — a tab is enough.

To be fair to Focus Friend

Name recognition is a real advantage and PomoMons doesn't have it: Focus Friend launched to Hank Green's existing audience and became the most-downloaded focus app in the country within weeks. And if your actual distraction is your phone — picking it up without thinking, scrolling before you've decided to — Focus Friend is solving exactly that problem, directly, by watching for it. PomoMons can't do that: it lives in a browser tab and has no way to know what you're doing on your phone, or in any other tab, for that matter. If phone-checking is the habit you're trying to break, a tool that watches your phone is doing something a browser tab structurally cannot.

What PomoMons is for is the other half of that same problem: the moment you sit down to work and a different tab is right there. That's a browser habit, and the fix for it has to live in the browser too.
